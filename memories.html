Selected Observations

Things that stuck with me

Some of these were probably ordinary moments to you. That is partly why I remember them.

Exhibit A — “the least we could do”

This happened something like a year and a half ago, and I don't think I've ever told you I remembered it. I was watching a homeless woman take cardboard from the dumpster and mentioned what I was seeing. Your reaction was that helping was “the least we could do.”

I had made an observation. You almost immediately converted it into a question about what we ought to do.

By calling an action “the least” one could do, the speaker distinguishes mere physical possibility from normative adequacy. Doing nothing remained physically available. It apparently did not count as an acceptable minimum.

Exhibit B — that produce guy

Remember that produce guy who would show up absurdly early? One time I basically just repeated what I had heard somebody else say about him, and your response was essentially: “he's just trying to do his job.”

The obvious part is that his timing made the shift more annoying. But that is not really why I remember your response.

It wasn't only extra work for you. On the days I worked that part of the shift, I complained too. Other people complained. I understood why they did. There was real substance to the frustration.

And you worked that position more than I did.

That is what made your response stand out. You had at least as much reason as anyone else to be irritated, probably more, and yet your attention went immediately to the fact that he was a person trying to do his job.

So the thing I remember is not merely that you were willing to tolerate an inconvenience. It is that, in a situation where basically everyone else - including me - had an intelligible complaint, you still made room in your judgment for the wellbeing and circumstances of the person everyone was complaining about.

There were also real stakes behind the complaints: people were not merely expressing irritation in a vacuum. Complaints can affect how someone's work is viewed, whether they retain a route or job, and how they are treated. Your instinct seemed unusually conscientious of that other person's position even while you were one of the people most inconvenienced.

I think that distinguished you from the rest of us more than you probably realized at the time.

Exhibit C — near the walk-in fridge

I remember standing near the walk-in fridge door feeling kind of bad that I was even asking you to cover me, because it wasn't an emergency. I asked whether you could cover me.

“Is everything okay?”

That was your first response. You didn't even know why I was asking yet.

This may have been a completely ordinary moment to you - just you being you. It landed much more profoundly on me than you probably had any reason to expect, and I remember it.
